Every ball in Big Alloy Merge carries an angular velocity and can spin freely around its center. When two balls collide, the tangential friction impulse at the contact point generates a torque that accelerates both balls' rotation — a ball that gets sideswiped will visibly spin. Rotation then decays over time through angular damping and is suppressed when a ball rubs against a wall. The system was introduced in v2.0.0.0 and significantly revised in v2.0.1.0 and v2.3.0.0.

## Rotation Parameters

| Parameter | Value (v2.3) | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `ANGULAR_DAMPING` | 0.92 | Fraction of angular velocity retained per second (exponential decay) |
| `WALL_ROTATIONAL_FRICTION` | 0.40 | Fraction of rotation suppressed each frame a ball touches a wall |
| `WALL_TORQUE_COUPLING` | 0.015 | Coefficient converting linear wall-contact velocity into new torque |
| Collision torque multiplier | 1.0 | Scales the torque generated by ball-to-ball collisions (reduced from 2.0 in v2.2) |
| Angular zero threshold | 0.005 rad/s | Angular velocity below this value is clamped to zero |

## Angular Damping

Angular damping uses a frame-rate-independent exponential formula introduced in v2.0.1.0:

```
ω *= damping ** dt
```

With `damping = 0.92`, a ball retains **85% of its spin after 1 second** and roughly 44% after 5 seconds. This is meaningfully different from the old per-frame formula (`ω *= 0.98`), which was both frame-rate-dependent and decayed too quickly at high FPS.

## Collision Torque

When `resolve_collision()` applies a tangential friction impulse between two balls, it simultaneously updates each ball's angular velocity using the following formulas:

```
cross = nx·ty - ny·tx
Δω₁ = f_impulse × ratio₁ × cross × 2 / r₁
Δω₂ = f_impulse × ratio₂ × cross × 2 / r₂
```

Where:
- `nx, ny` — contact normal (unit vector from ball 2 to ball 1)
- `tx, ty` — contact tangent (unit vector along the sliding direction)
- `cross` — the scalar cross product `nx·ty - ny·tx`, equal to ±1, capturing the sign of the torque
- `ratio₁, ratio₂` — mass-based displacement ratios (`m_other / (m1 + m2)`)
- `r₁, r₂` — ball radii (the lever arm of the torque)
- `f_impulse` — the magnitude of the Coulomb-clamped tangential impulse

The `2 / r` factor comes from the moment of inertia of a solid disk (`I = ½·m·r²`), where `τ = f × r` and `Δω = τ / I`.

## Wall Friction Behavior

Wall contact produces two rotational effects applied in sequence each time a ball touches a wall.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Suppress existing rotation">
    The ball's current angular velocity is reduced:
    ```
    ω *= (1 - WALL_ROTATIONAL_FRICTION)
    ```
    With `WALL_ROTATIONAL_FRICTION = 0.40`, 40% of the spin is removed on contact.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply linear-to-angular coupling">
    If the ball's linear speed exceeds 1.0 px/frame, a small new torque is added based on how fast the ball is moving along the wall surface. The direction depends on which wall is contacted:
    - **Left wall:** downward motion generates counter-clockwise torque — `Δω += vy × μ_w × tc / r`
    - **Right wall:** downward motion generates clockwise torque — `Δω -= vy × μ_w × tc / r`
    - **Floor:** horizontal motion generates torque in the opposing direction — `Δω -= vx × μ_w × tc / r`
    - **Ceiling:** rotation is suppressed but no new torque is added
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Version History

| Version | Change |
|---|---|
| v2.0.0.0 | Rotation physics first introduced — collision torque, per-frame angular decay (`ω *= 0.98`), wall coupling coefficient 1.0 |
| v2.0.1.0 | Major fix — frame-rate-independent decay (`ω *= damping ** dt`), `WALL_ROTATIONAL_FRICTION` added, `WALL_TORQUE_COUPLING` reduced 1.0 → 0.08 to eliminate wild spinning |
| v2.3.0.0 | Rotation slowed further — `ANGULAR_DAMPING` 0.98 → 0.92, collision torque multiplier 2.0 → 1.0, `WALL_TORQUE_COUPLING` 0.03 → 0.015 |
